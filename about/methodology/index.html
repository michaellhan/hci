Methodology

In short

  • Where facts come from: Harvard pages, clubs' own websites and recruiting pages, student newspapers, and student reports that a person has reviewed. Never from memory or guesswork.
  • Why so much says “Unknown”: most clubs don't publish their time commitment, size or dues. When no source states a fact, we say so instead of filling it in — and ask anyone who knows to tell us.
  • Checking a fact: on a club page, tap any fact to see the page it came from and when we last saw it there. Every source is listed at the bottom of the page.
  • When sources disagree: we keep every version and show the one from the more official, more recent source, marked “sources differ”. More below.
  • Dates: deadlines belong to a semester. “Usually recruits in the fall” is a pattern from past recruiting, never a promise about this term. Open or closed is worked out from the dates on the day you look.
  • AI: a language model helps read clubs' own pages, but a fact is kept only when the exact words appear on the page (numbers and dates included). It also writes one-line summaries from a club's own text and suggests categories, which are labelled as estimates.
  • No rankings: we don't rate clubs or estimate how hard they are to get into. An acceptance rate appears only when a club published both how many applied and how many were admitted.
  • Corrections: every club page has a “Help improve this page” form. A person checks every answer before it appears. See About for takedowns.

How a fact is kept

Every fact is stored with where it came from: one source saying one thing about a club, for example “the SEAS student-organization page lists this club's website as X” or “the club's join page says applications close October 3.” Each claim keeps the page it came from, when we saw it, the exact quote where there is one, and how it was read. What you see on a club page is chosen from those by fixed rules, the same for every club.

Kinds of evidence

Club pages show facts plainly; tap one to see where it came from, and every source is in the page's Sources list. Values only students reported are labelled student-reported, and estimated categories are labelled as estimates.

The labels we use behind the scenes
  • VerifiedStated by an official Harvard or club source, recently observed.
  • ReportedStated by a secondary source, or an official source we haven't re-checked recently.
  • CommunityReported by students; not yet confirmed by an official source.
  • ComputedCalculated from sourced numbers.
  • EstimateInferred or derived by us — not directly stated by a source.
  • UnknownNo acceptable evidence yet.

When sources disagree

We keep every version and show one: the more official source wins (a club's own page over a news article; a Harvard page over a blog), and for things that change — deadlines, member counts — a more recent statement wins. The page then says “sources differ”, and tapping the fact lists what each source says. Deleting evidence is never how a conflict gets fixed; a wrong value is marked rejected, with a reason, and can be restored.

Source trust

Sources are ranked by who published them. A club's own website or social account counts as official only once ownership is established: a Harvard page links to it, an already-established club channel does, or a site that names the club in its own title is also linked under the club's name by someone else — a Harvard publication, Wikipedia's article on the club, or two other sites. A matching name alone never counts. An Instagram account found by searching for the club's name stays unverified: we still link to it, but nothing it says counts as official.

Confidence is computed as base (by tier) × extraction method × directness, never assigned by hand. Parsing a page scores 0.95; a language model's answer scores 0.85 and is accepted only with a verbatim quote from the page. Anything inferred rather than stated is halved. Independent sources that agree raise the displayed confidence.

Joining: what we can conclude

Sources rarely say everything at once, so we draw a few careful conclusions from what they do say, and each one opens to the pages it rests on. We never write a quote a source didn't say.

  • Open membership means a source says anyone can join, or that you become a member by showing up, and no source names an application, comp, audition or tryout for general members.
  • A process stated only for some roles (board and leadership roles, competition teams, performing roles, running the club's fund) doesn't make general membership selective. An open club with a selective board is still open.
  • No comp comes from the College's student-organization directory, which lists a “no comping requirement” flag, or from open membership. The flag says nothing about an application or an audition, so we don't treat it as open membership.
  • First-years eligible means a source names first-years, lists the class years that can join, or opens membership to everyone or all Harvard undergraduates, which includes first-years. “Sophomores and up” or a punch for sophomores and juniors means not eligible. A specific rule (“first apply in your sophomore year”) outweighs a general one (“any undergraduate may apply”), and the profile lists both.
  • A no-cut comp is one a source says admits everyone who completes it.
  • When sources disagree, the fact is shown as unclear and left out of filters. A club that says nothing is never counted as a “no”: a filter shows confirmed matches, and the directory says how many clubs don't say.

Deadlines and semesters

Recruiting information belongs to a specific semester, for example Fall 2026, not to the club forever. Relative dates like “next Friday” are resolved only against the date we fetched the page, and a date with no time is never silently turned into 11:59 PM. Whether a cycle is open is computed from its dates each time you load the page. An “applications are open” with no dates counts as open only for three weeks after we last saw it.

Competitiveness

We never estimate an acceptance rate. A rate appears only when a club published both an applicant count and an admit count for a named semester, and we show it next to the two numbers it was computed from. A label is added only with at least 10 applicants: highly selective, selective, moderately selective, admits most applicants. When a club says its process is “very competitive,” we quote the club; we don't turn that into a number. Having a comp or an application says nothing about how selective it is.

Active or dormant?

Activity status is an estimate, shown with the reasons behind it. It uses signals like a current Harvard listing, a dated recruiting cycle, or a recently updated website. Missing signals lower confidence; they are not proof a club is gone.

Duplicates

The same club often appears in several directories under slightly different names. We merge records automatically only on a strong shared identifier, like the same official website or directory ID. Similar names go to a person for review. Every merge is logged and can be undone.
